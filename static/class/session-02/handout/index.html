Glossary

The 32 session words
plus intrapreneurship

One word for each session, in course order. Definitions come from the key terms and text of the book.

01Entrepreneurship Seeing a need that is not being met, gathering the resources to meet it, and carrying the result, including the risk of failure. (Sternberg, 2026, Part 1)

02Chaos What happens when you do not know why things are going wrong: unknown numbers, unclear decisions, hidden disagreements or a missed part of the business. (Sternberg, 2026, Part 1)

03Owner The person who carries the result of the business, including the blame. You can be an owner without owning shares. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 01)

04Strategy A small set of choices about who you serve, what you offer and why customers pick you. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 01)

05Opportunity A need, a buyer who will pay to meet it, and a way for you to meet it better than the current option. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 02)

06Gap A need that every current supplier misses. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 02)

07Resources Everything a business can use: money, time, skills, tools, space, materials and the people you know. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 03)

08Bootstrapping Building a business with your own money and early sales, without outside investors. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 03)

09Bet Any commitment of money, time or reputation whose result you cannot know in advance. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 04)

10Scenario A believable story about one possible future, used to test a plan. A scenario is not a forecast. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 04)

11Question An open question cannot be answered with yes or no, such as “What happened?” or “Tell me more.” (Sternberg, 2026, Section 05)

12Evidence What people actually did, counted or recorded. Opinions are weak evidence; behavior is strong evidence. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 05)

13Failure A result that does not meet the goal. Some failures are cheap and useful; others are expensive and teach nothing. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 06)

14Pivot A deliberate change of direction based on what you learned. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 06; Ries, 2011)

15Focus Putting limited time, money and attention on a few chosen things. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 07)

16Priority The thing that comes first when you cannot do everything. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 07)

17Feedback Information from what customers do and say that tells you what to change next. (Sternberg, 2026, Part 1)

18Hypothesis A guess stated so clearly that a test can prove it wrong. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 05)

19Sale The conversation that turns a buyer’s readiness to listen into a yes. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 08)

20Channel The path a product takes to the customer: your own shop, a market stall, a website, a distributor or a marketplace. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 08)

21Margin The share of the price left after costs. Gross margin subtracts only the product’s cost; net margin subtracts all costs. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 09)

22Runway How many months your cash will last at your current spending. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 09)

23Team A small group with a shared goal, shared responsibility for the result and members who depend on each other. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 10; Hackman, 2002)

24Conflict Task conflict is disagreement about the work, which can help. Relationship conflict is personal, and it hurts. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 10; Jehn, 1995)

25Bottleneck The one step, part or supplier that limits how fast the whole operation can move. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 11; Goldratt & Cox, 1984)

26System Work you repeat, turned into written routines, so the business runs well even when you are not in the room. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 11)

27Product What the customer gets: a physical item, a service, software or a mix. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 12)

28Prototype An early, rough working version used to learn. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 12)

29Plan Your best current guess about how the business will work. Treat it as a hypothesis, and change it when the evidence is clear. (Sternberg, 2026, Section 05)

30Rehearsal Practicing your presentation in front of others before the real one, to get feedback and fix it. (Sternberg, 2026, Part 5)

31Pitch A short case for your idea, made to the people who would have to say yes. It is the cheapest test of an idea. (Sternberg, 2026, Part 5)

32Starve To starve chaos: stop feeding it guesses, vague plans, hidden disagreements and numbers you have not checked. (Sternberg, 2026, Part 1)

+Intrapreneurship Bringing a successful entrepreneur’s skills and mindset inside a large organization. (Sternberg, 2026, Part 1; Pinchot, 1985)
